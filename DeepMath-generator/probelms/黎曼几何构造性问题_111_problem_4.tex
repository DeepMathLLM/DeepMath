problem:  
Let \((M,g,V)\) be a complete smooth \(n\)-dimensional weighted Riemannian manifold, \(\mu=e^{-V}\,d\mathrm{vol}_g\), and \((P_t)_{t\ge0}\) the diffusion semigroup generated by \(\Delta_V=\Delta-\nabla V\cdot\nabla\).  
For each \(x\in M\) and unit vector \(v\in T_xM\), let \(U_t\colon T_xM\to T_{X_t}M\) be stochastic parallel transport along the diffusion \((X_t)\) starting at \(x\).  
Define the **finite-time stochastic Ricci functional**
\[
\mathcal{R}_t(x,v):=\mathbb{E}_x[\mathrm{Ric}_V(X_t)(U_t v,U_t v)].
\]

Suppose that for a fixed finite collection of short times \(0<t_1<\cdots<t_m \ll1\), the quantities \(\mathcal{R}_{t_i}(x,v)\) are accessible (in principle measured or computed) for all \(x,v\).  
No information beyond these finitely many observations is available, and in particular the infinitesimal limit \(t\to0\) is not assumed.

**Question (research-level open problem):**  
Does there exist any quantitative procedure—possibly depending on dimension, regularity of \(V\), and geometric bounds on \((M,g)\)—that reconstructs a meaningful lower Bakry–Émery curvature bound solely from the discrete data \(\{\mathcal{R}_{t_i}(x,v)\}_{i=1}^{m}\)?  In particular:

- Can one find an explicit function \(\Phi_{n,m}(t_1,\dots,t_m)\to0\) as all \(t_i\to0\) such that
  \[
  \mathrm{Ric}_V(x)(v,v)\ge
     \inf_i \mathcal{R}_{t_i}(x,v)-\Phi_{n,m}(t_1,\dots,t_m),
  \]
  or prove that no such finite reconstruction modulus exists?  
- More broadly, what minimal stochastic information (e.g. finitely many heat kernel derivatives, or short-time averages of \(\mathcal R_t\)) suffices to determine a lower curvature bound?

This is an **inverse diffusion–curvature problem** at finite resolution: can curvature be *recovered quantitatively* from finitely sampled diffusion-scale statistics?

Why is it a "good" problem:  
It transforms the classical curvature–diffusion equivalence into a genuinely **inverse reconstruction** question under discrete, finite-time constraints—a setting untouched by existing theory.  Classical results recover curvature from infinitesimal (continuous in \(t\)) diffusion data via small-time asymptotics or full semigroup inequalities; here one asks whether *limited stochastic observations* contain sufficient geometric information to deduce a global curvature bound.  
This problem lies at the intersection of geometric analysis, stochastic processes, and inverse problems, potentially opening a mathematical framework for “statistical geometry’’—recovering curvature from partial diffusion flow data.  
The formulation is extremely simple yet conceptually deep: it inquires whether finite-time stochastic phenomena uniquely encode a local geometric invariant (curvature), embodying the “narrow entrance, profound depth’’ characteristic of a genuinely good research problem.

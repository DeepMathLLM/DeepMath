problem:  
Let \( M^m \hookrightarrow (\mathbb{S}^n, g_{\mathrm{std}}) \) be a **nondegenerate** compact minimal submanifold, meaning its Jacobi operator \(J_M\) on normal vector fields is invertible.  
For every smooth mean‑zero function \(u \in C^\infty(\mathbb{S}^n)\), consider the one‑parameter family of conformal metrics \( g_t = e^{2tu} g_{\mathrm{std}} \).  
By the implicit function theorem, there is a unique smooth family of immersions \( i_t: M \to \mathbb{S}^n \) such that \(M_t = i_t(M)\) is minimal with respect to \(g_t\), and \(i_0 = i\).  

Let \( \mathcal{A}_t = \operatorname{Vol}(M_t, g_t)\) denote the area functional of the corresponding minimal submanifold in the deformed geometry. The first and second derivatives of \(\mathcal{A}_t\) at \(t=0\) are determined by the conformal–minimal first and second variations, and \( \ddot{\mathcal{A}}_0 \) represents the coupled stability quadratic form previously studied.  

Define the *third‑order conformal–minimal response*  
\[
\mathcal{C}_M(u) := \left.\frac{d^3}{dt^3}\right|_{t=0} \mathcal{A}_t ,
\]
which, under nondegeneracy, is a well-defined cubic functional depending on \(u\), \(J_M^{-1}\), and the second fundamental form of \(M\).  

**Problem:**  
Does there exist a non‑totally‑geodesic minimal submanifold \( M \subset \mathbb{S}^n \) for which \( \mathcal{C}_M(u) \equiv 0 \) for all mean‑zero \( u \)?  
Equivalently, are totally geodesic submanifolds characterized as the only minimal submanifolds whose minimal volume is stationary up to third order under all conformal ambient variations?  
If not, what geometric conditions on \(M\) (e.g. symmetries, specific eigenfunction interactions of \(J_M\)) could enforce the vanishing of \(\mathcal{C}_M(u)\)?  

Why is it a "good" problem:  
This problem corrects the degeneracy and well‑posedness issues by assuming nondegeneracy, ensuring a unique analytic branch of minimal immersions under small conformal change. It introduces the first higher‑order (beyond the classical second variation) obstruction to conformal rigidity by defining a precise and intrinsic cubic functional \(\mathcal{C}_M(u)\).  
At the conceptual level, the problem probes whether “third‑order conformal neutrality” exists: that is, whether minimal submanifolds can remain volume‑stationary through three levels of conformal perturbation without being totally geodesic. Resolving this would connect analytic perturbation theory (through expansions of \(J_M^{-1} F_u\)), conformal geometry (behavior under Möbius transformations), and geometric rigidity.  
It thereby provides a clear, rigorous, and deep question—simple to state but tapping into uncharted territory at the intersection of geometric analysis and conformal invariance—making it an authentically good research‑level problem.

problem:  
Let $\mathbb{B}^{3}\subset \mathbb{R}^{3}$ denote the closed unit ball with boundary $\mathbb{S}^{2}$.  
For each smooth embedded Jordan curve $\Gamma\subset \mathbb{S}^{2}$, let $\mathcal{S}(\Gamma)$ denote the set of **stable minimal disks** $M\subset \mathbb{B}^{3}$ satisfying $\partial M=\Gamma$.  
Consider the parameter space $\mathcal{C}$ of smooth embedded circles in $\mathbb{S}^{2}$ (with the $C^{2,\alpha}$ topology), and define the moduli space of pairs
\[
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{st}} = \{\, (\Gamma, M) : M \in \mathcal{S}(\Gamma)\, \} \subset \mathcal{C} \times \mathbb{B}^{3}.
\]  
Let $\pi:\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{st}}\to \mathcal{C}$ be the natural projection, and let $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{st}}^0$ denote the component containing a nondegenerate minimal disk $(\Gamma_0,M_0)$.

**Question:**  
Is there a generic open subset $\mathcal{U}\subset \mathcal{C}$ such that $\pi:\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{st}}^0\to \mathcal{U}$ is locally a smooth submersion but fails to be globally injective?  
In particular, can *global bifurcation* occur without loss of stability—i.e., can there exist two distinct families  
\[
\{M_t\}_{t\in[0,1]} ,\quad \{M'_t\}_{t\in[0,1]}
\]
of stable minimal disks with $\partial M_t = \partial M'_t = \Gamma_t$ for all $t$, with $M_0=M'_0$ but $M_1\neq M'_1$, even though no Jacobi degeneracy arises along either branch?  

Equivalently: can nontrivial *stable multi-valued continuation* of the Plateau solution occur, representing a global topological obstruction in the moduli projection $\pi$?

Why is it a "good" problem:  
This version eliminates the ill-defined covering-space formalism and makes the question an **analytic bifurcation vs. topology** problem in the moduli of minimal surfaces. It asks whether *global nonuniqueness among stable branches* of minimal disks can occur **without local linear degeneracy**, which is currently unknown and requires a synthesis of global geometric analysis and nonlinear PDE theory.  

The problem targets a precise and natural tension between **local elliptic regularity** (implying uniqueness near nondegenerate points) and **global topological or geometric obstructions** that could break uniqueness on large scales. It is conceptually simple—“can stable minimal disks bifurcate globally under smoothly varying boundaries?”—yet any resolution would demand new tools uniting analytic continuation, global minimal-surface moduli topology, and stability theory.  

This question thus embodies a profound and clear open direction at the heart of minimal surface analysis and moduli geometry, striking the right balance of simplicity, depth, and cross-disciplinary significance.

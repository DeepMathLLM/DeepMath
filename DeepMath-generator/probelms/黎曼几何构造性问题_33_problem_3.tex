problem:  
Let \( f: (M^{m}, g) \to (N^{n}, h) \) be a **horizontally conformal harmonic morphism** between compact oriented Riemannian manifolds with connected minimal fibers.  Denote by \(A\) and \(T\) the O’Neill tensors of the harmonic submersion \(f\), measuring respectively the nonintegrability of the horizontal distribution and the deviation of fibers from total geodesicity.  

Assume that \(h\) has **positive sectional curvature** and that \(M\) has **nonnegative Ricci curvature**.  

**Conjectural Rigidity Problem:**  
Is it true that if the **horizontal distribution** has bounded nonintegrability in the sense that  
\[
\int_M |A|^2 \, d\mathrm{vol}_g < \varepsilon
\]
for some sufficiently small universal constant \(\varepsilon>0\) (depending only on \(m,n\) and curvature bounds of \(N\)), then the map \(f\) must be (up to scaling and covering) a **Riemannian submersion with integrable horizontal distribution**, i.e. \(A \equiv 0\), and its fibers are totally geodesic?  

Equivalently, does a small \(L^2\)-norm of the O’Neill tensor \(A\) force a harmonic morphism from a nonnegatively Ricci-curved manifold onto a positively curved manifold to be locally a Riemannian product submersion?  

Why is it a "good" problem:  
This problem directly targets the **quantitative rigidity** of harmonic morphisms—the idea that sufficiently small geometric “twisting” (measured by the horizontal nonintegrability tensor \(A\)) should be impossible unless it vanishes entirely under combined curvature conditions on base and domain.  

It is “good” because:  

1. **Bridges Geometry and Analysis:** The condition \(\int |A|^2 < \varepsilon\) is analytic and variational in nature, while the desired conclusion (vanishing of \(A\)) is geometric and topological. A resolution would require creating new analytic estimates—Bochner-type or energy-gap arguments—relating curvature of \(M,N\) to the quadratic tensor \(A\).  

2. **Extends Classic Rigidity Themes:** While total vanishing of \(A\) under positive curvature is known to imply strong geometric product structures, the introduction of a *quantitative smallness threshold* opens a new avenue: it asks whether *approximate integrability* forces *exact integrability* under curvature pinching—reminiscent of quantitative sphere theorems and gap phenomena in Ricci flow or minimal surface theory.  

3. **Potential for Deep Analytical Tools:** Attempting to prove such an “energy gap” would likely require coupling harmonic map PDE estimates with nonlinear curvature identities of Riemannian submersions, melding harmonic analysis, global geometry, and possibly spectral theory.  

4. **Satisfies “Simplicity with Depth”:** The statement is clean and compact—just one inequality involving \(|A|\)—but it encodes the heart of geometric-analysis interaction between curvature, fiber geometry, and harmonicity.  

Thus, the problem challenges one to discover whether there exists a **quantitative rigidity threshold** for harmonic morphisms under curvature positivity—a conceptually elegant and potentially far-reaching question that could inaugurate a new class of geometric gap phenomena.

problem:  
Let \((M,g,V)\) be a complete smooth \(n\)-dimensional weighted Riemannian manifold with measure  
\(\mu = e^{-V}\,d\mathrm{vol}_g\) and Bakry–Émery curvature \(\mathrm{Ric}_V = \mathrm{Ric}_g + \nabla^2V\).  
For a fixed averaging radius \(r_0>0\), define the **mesoscopic Bakry–Émery curvature tensor** by a canonical exponential-map construction:
\[
\overline{\mathrm{Ric}}^{(r_0)}_V(x)(v,v)
:= \frac{\int_{B_{r_0}(0_x)} 
      \langle \mathrm{Ric}_V(\exp_x(w))(\Pi_{w} v),\Pi_{w} v\rangle\,
      J_x(w)\, e^{-V(\exp_x(w))} \,dw}
      {\int_{B_{r_0}(0_x)} J_x(w)\, e^{-V(\exp_x(w))}\,dw},
\]
where \(J_x(w)\) is the Jacobian determinant of \(\exp_x\) and \(\Pi_w\colon T_xM\to T_{\exp_x(w)}M\) is parallel transport along the unique geodesic \(\gamma(t)=\exp_x(tw)\).  
This definition avoids ambiguities from the cut locus and is smooth for small \(r_0\).

Assume that for some constant \(K>0\),
\[
\overline{\mathrm{Ric}}^{(r_0)}_V(x)(v,v)\ge K|v|^2
\quad \text{for all }x\in M\text{ and all unit }v\in T_xM.
\]

Let \((P_t)_{t>0}\) be the diffusion semigroup generated by the weighted Laplacian \(\Delta_V = \Delta - \nabla V\cdot\nabla\), and denote by \(X_t\) the associated diffusion process.  
For each smooth \(f\), define its **coarse carré du champ** averaged at scale \(r_0\):
\[
\Gamma_t^{(r_0)}(f)(x)
  := \frac{\int_{B_{r_0}(0_x)} 
         \frac{1}{2t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\exp_x(w)}[(f(X_t)-f(X_0))^2]\,
         J_x(w)e^{-V(\exp_x(w))}\,dw}
       {\int_{B_{r_0}(0_x)}J_x(w)e^{-V(\exp_x(w))}\,dw}.
\]

**Question (research-level open problem):**  
Given this fixed-scale curvature lower bound,
does there exist a universal modulus \(\varepsilon(t,r_0,n)\to 0\) as \(t\to 0\) such that the following *mesoscopic Bakry–Émery inequality* holds for all smooth \(f\)?
\[
\Gamma_{2t}^{(r_0)}(f)(x)
   \le e^{-2Kt+\varepsilon(t,r_0,n)}\,
        \Gamma_t^{(r_0)}(f)(x), \qquad \forall x\in M.
\]
Equivalently, can small-time semigroup convexity (or hypercontractivity) be quantitatively recovered when the Bakry–Émery curvature bound is known only after averaging over a fixed radius \(r_0\)?  
Is the curvature–diffusion correspondence *stable under mesoscopic geometric averaging*?

Why is it a "good" problem:  
This problem probes a frontier question at the interface of geometry and analysis: How much local geometric information is actually needed to enforce analytic curvature–dimension inequalities?  Classical Bakry–Émery theory requires pointwise tensor bounds, while global comparison results control integrated geometric quantities; the mesoscopic regime—curvature controlled only after fixed-scale averaging—is an open and unexplored zone in between.  
The problem’s formulation is conceptually simple yet deeply integrative: it connects averaged Ricci geometry (as in integral Ricci bounds à la Petersen–Wei and Aubry) with fine short-time stochastic behavior of diffusions. Proving or disproving such an inequality would pioneer a new form of curvature–diffusion stability, bridging coarse geometric analysis, probabilistic semigroup methods, and heat kernel asymptotics.  
It exemplifies the “narrow entrance, profound depth’’ criterion: a minimal change to well-known hypotheses (average instead of pointwise curvature) leads to a question demanding potentially new analytic, geometric, and stochastic ideas.

problem:  
Let \((X,d,m)\) be a compact \(\mathrm{RCD}(K,N)\) space with \(K>0\) and \(N<\infty\).  
Assume that \((X,d,m)\) admits a **metric-measure foliation** in the sense of Cavalletti–Mondino or Gigli–Pasqualetto: that is, there exists a quotient map  
\[
\pi:(X,d,m)\to (Z,d_Z,m_Z)
\]
such that \(m=\int_Z m_z\,dm_Z(z)\), each fiber \((X_z,d,m_z)\) is an \(\mathrm{RCD}(K',N')\) space for some \(K'>0\), \(N'<\infty\), and \(\pi\) is a metric submersion (i.e. the quotient satisfies the correct infinitesimal Hilbertian structure).  

**Problem:**  
Suppose moreover that \(Z\) is an RCD\((K_Z,k)\) space admitting a biLipschitz embedding into \(\mathbb{R}^k\) (for example, \(Z\cong \mathbb{R}^k\) in the smooth case).  
Is it true that, up to measured Gromov–Hausdorff isomorphism,  
\[
(X,d,m)\ \simeq\ (Y,d_Y,m_Y) \times (Z,d_Z,m_Z)
\]
for some \(\mathrm{RCD}(K'',N'+k)\) space \(Y\) with \(K''\ge 0\)?  
Equivalently, does the presence of a *metric‑measure foliation with positively curved fibers and Euclidean‑type base* enforce a global product decomposition in the sense of synthetic lower Ricci curvature?

Why is it a "good" problem:  
This version poses a precise rigidity question within the established RCD framework. It unites several active areas:  
- the *metric‑measure foliation theory*, which generalizes Riemannian submersions and quotient constructions to the synthetic setting;  
- the *RCD splitting and rigidity phenomena*, generalizing de Rham and Cheeger–Gromoll splittings;  
- the geometry of *spaces with positive and nonnegative Ricci curvature bounds*.  

A positive answer would signal a powerful extension of de Rham–type decomposition to the non‑smooth world, revealing that synthetic lower Ricci bounds still enforce strong global structure when curvature interacts correctly along foliations. A counterexample would illuminate the precise limits of this synthesis.  
The statement is conceptually simple—does a regular foliation with positively curved leaves over a flat base force a product?—but in depth it touches the core of current geometric analysis and optimal transport rigidity theories, qualifying it as a profound, generative research problem.

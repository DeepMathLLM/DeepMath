problem:  
Let \((M^n, g)\) be a compact Riemannian manifold with smooth boundary \(\partial M\), \(n \ge 3\).  
For the conformally covariant GJMS operator \(P_{2k,g}\) of order \(2k\) on the interior of \(M\) (\(k \in \mathbb{N}\)), it is known that on closed manifolds (\(\partial M = \emptyset\)) these operators exist and are unique for all admissible \(k\) (up to normalization) when \(n>2k\). On manifolds with boundary, Graham–Zworski’s scattering theory constructs associated nonlocal operators \((P_{2k,g}, P_{2k,g}^{\text{bdry}})\) in certain asymptotically hyperbolic settings, where \(P_{2k,g}^{\text{bdry}}\) acts on the boundary and generalizes the fractional Laplacian.  

**Open problem:**  
For *compact* Riemannian manifolds with *smooth boundary* (not assumed conformally compact or asymptotically hyperbolic), does there exist a canonical family of *local or pseudodifferential* conformally covariant operator pairs
\[
(P_{2k,g}, B_{2k,g}) \text{ on } (M,g),
\]
of interior order \(2k\) and boundary order \(2k-1\), satisfying:

1. \(P_{2k,g}\) reduces to the GJMS operator on closed manifolds,  
2. \((P_{2k,g}, B_{2k,g})\) is elliptically coercive and self-adjoint with natural spectral boundary conditions,  
3. the system is variational, i.e. arises as the Euler–Lagrange equations of a conformally invariant energy of order \(2k\),  
4. the pair depends naturally on \(g\) (no auxiliary asymptotic compactification required).

Equivalently, can one construct a **fully intrinsic boundary version** of the GJMS hierarchy extending Escobar’s second-order conformal Laplacian–boundary operator pair to all even orders, without recourse to asymptotically hyperbolic (scattering) geometry?  

Why is it a “good” problem:  
This problem cleanly formulates a major open frontier in conformal and geometric analysis: defining intrinsic higher-order conformally covariant boundary operators beyond the classical Yamabe–Escobar (second-order) case. Its formulation is simple but its depth is immense, lying at the junction of conformal geometry, analysis on manifolds with boundary, and nonlocal scattering theory. A solution would produce a universal boundary extension of the GJMS program, bridging compact and conformally compact settings, and unifying the analytic, geometric, and variational theories of conformal invariants. Conversely, a proof of nonexistence would identify essential obstructions to extending conformal covariance beyond known constructions. In either direction, it is a sharp, profound, and highly consequential question—an archetypal “good” research problem.

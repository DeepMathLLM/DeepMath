problem:  
Let \( (M, g) \) be a compact, simply connected hyperkähler manifold of real dimension \(4n\), and let  
\[
(Z, \pi: Z \to \mathbb{CP}^1, \sigma, \mathcal{C})
\]
be its **twistor structure**: \(Z\) is a complex manifold of complex dimension \(2n+1\), \(\pi\) a holomorphic submersion, \(\sigma\) an antiholomorphic involution covering the antipodal map, and \(\mathcal{C}\) the family of real sections of \(\pi\) with normal bundle \( \mathcal{O}(1)^{2n} \) corresponding to the points of \(M\).  
By the Hitchin–Karlhede–Lindström–Roček correspondence, \( (Z, \pi, \sigma, \mathcal{C}) \) determines \( (M, g) \) up to hyperkähler isometry.

**Problem.**  
Let \( (Z, \pi, \sigma) \) be a complex manifold with the twistor-type conditions above, compatible with some hyperkähler structure. Investigate whether the family of real \(\sigma\)-invariant sections with normal bundle \( \mathcal{O}(1)^{2n} \) inside \(Z\) is **unique** up to holomorphic automorphism of \(Z\) commuting with \(\pi\) and \(\sigma\).  

Equivalently, can two distinct smooth, \(\sigma\)-invariant families \(\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{C}' \subset \mathrm{Douady}(Z)\) of \( \mathcal{O}(1)^{2n} \)-sections correspond to non-isometric hyperkähler metrics on the same differentiable manifold \(M\)?  
Or does the **complex and real-analytic geometry of the twistor space alone** rigidly determine the corresponding hyperkähler metric, even before specifying which family of twistor lines is chosen?  

Why is it a "good" problem:  
This problem removes the earlier dimensional inconsistency and asks a precise rigidity question at the intersection of **deformation theory**, **hyperkähler geometry**, and **twistor theory**. The HKLR correspondence reconstructs the metric from the full pair \((Z, \mathcal{C})\), but it leaves an open conceptual gap: are there potentially multiple inequivalent real families of \( \mathcal{O}(1)^{2n} \)-sections inside a single twistor space \(Z\)? Current deformation theory (via the Kodaira theory of deformations of rational curves) ensures local smoothness near a given section but does not rule out the existence of *other* connected components of the Douady space carrying the same normal bundle structure.  

Establishing uniqueness—or constructing a counterexample—would determine whether the **complex twistor space** already encodes the full hyperkähler structure or whether the embedding of twistor lines carries additional geometric information. Such a result would clarify the precise mapping between differential-geometric moduli of hyperkähler metrics and the complex-analytic moduli of twistor spaces, bridging Riemannian holonomy, complex geometry, and deformation theory.  

Its statement is succinct, the concepts involved are fundamental, and the resolution would have far-reaching impact on the foundations of special holonomy and moduli theory. Hence it constitutes a mathematically sound, deeply probing, and “good” research-level problem.

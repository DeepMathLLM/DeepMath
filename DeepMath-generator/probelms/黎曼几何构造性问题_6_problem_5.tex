problem:  
Let \( M_i^n \subset S^{n+k} \) be compact, smooth, **embedded minimal submanifolds** satisfying the scale‑invariant bounds
\[
\int_{M_i} |A_{M_i}|^n \le C,\qquad \mathrm{Vol}(M_i) \le V_0.
\]
Assume that, viewed as Riemannian manifolds with the induced metrics \(g_{M_i}\), the sequence exhibits **partial core collapse** in the following explicit sense: for every \(\varepsilon>0\) there is a covering of \(M_i\) by two disjoint open sets \(U_i^\mathrm{reg}, U_i^\mathrm{coll}\) such that

- \(\mathrm{inj}_{M_i}(p)\ge \varepsilon\) on \(U_i^\mathrm{reg}\),  
- \(\mathrm{Vol}(U_i^\mathrm{coll},g_{M_i})\to 0\) as \(i\to\infty\).

  
**Question (Partial collapse vs. curvature concentration at the critical scale):**  
Under these assumptions, must all the curvature energy  
\(\int_{M_i}|A|^n\) asymptotically concentrate in the collapsing region \(U_i^\mathrm{coll}\)?  Equivalently, does any loss of injectivity radius at the critical total curvature bound necessarily correlate with curvature concentration, i.e.
\[
\lim_{\delta\to0}\limsup_{i\to\infty}\int_{\{p\in M_i:\mathrm{inj}_{M_i}(p)<\delta\}} |A_{M_i}|^n = 
\int_{M_i}|A_{M_i}|^n - \lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{U_i^\mathrm{reg}} |A_{M_i}|^n ?
\]
Or can there exist sequences with bounded \(\int |A|^n\) where intrinsic collapse occurs (injectivity radius \(\to0\)) while curvature energy remains **uniformly distributed**, yielding collapse **without** concentration?

Why is it a "good" problem:  
This version removes ambiguous structures and formulates a crisp analytic–geometric challenge: can intrinsic collapse of minimal submanifolds occur without curvature focusing, under the exact critical curvature control?  The problem lies at the **intersection of geometric measure theory, elliptic regularity, and collapse geometry**:

- If curvature concentration is unavoidable, one could quantify collapse purely through curvature energy, extending the bubble‑tree paradigm to an **energy–injectivity** dichotomy.
- If non‑concentrating collapse is possible, it would reveal a new degeneration mechanism for minimal submanifolds analogous to volume collapse with bounded Ricci curvature, but driven here by extrinsic geometry.

The formulation is elementary yet subtle—it asks whether every region of vanishing injectivity at the analytic threshold must carry significant curvature, or whether truly flat‑but‑collapsed minimal geometries can exist.  Resolving either outcome would clarify the analytic link between extrinsic curvature energy and intrinsic geometry, making it a precise, open, and conceptually deep research problem.

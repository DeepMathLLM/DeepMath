problem:  
Let \( (M,g) \) be a compact, simply connected, irreducible hyperkähler manifold of real dimension \(4n\), and let   
\[
(Z, J_Z, \pi: Z \to \mathbb{CP}^1, \sigma)
\]
be its associated twistor space endowed with projection \(\pi\) and the real structure \(\sigma\) covering the antipodal map.  

Let \(\mathrm{Def}_{HK}(M)\) denote the local (verse) of hyperkähler metric deformations of \((M,g)\) modulo diffeomorphisms isotopic to the identity, and let \(\mathrm{Def}_{Tw}(Z;\pi,\sigma)\) denote the local Kuranishi space of complex deformations of \(Z\) *preserving the holomorphic submersion \(\pi\) and the real involution \(\sigma\)* (up to biholomorphic equivalences respecting these).  

**Problem.**  
Is the natural map  
\[
\Phi:\mathrm{Def}_{HK}(M)\longrightarrow \mathrm{Def}_{Tw}(Z;\pi,\sigma)
\]
that sends a deformation of the hyperkähler metric to the corresponding twistor deformation a *local isomorphism of deformation spaces* universally for all compact irreducible hyperkähler manifolds?  

Equivalently, do all infinitesimal complex deformations of \((Z,J_Z)\) preserving \((\pi,\sigma)\) arise from actual deformations of the hyperkähler metric on \(M\)?  
If this fails, exhibit or rule out the existence of genuine “twistor-only” complex deformations—complex structures on \(Z\) that continue to admit compatible \((\pi,\sigma)\) but do not correspond to metric deformations of a hyperkähler \(M\).  

Why is it a "good" problem:  
This formulation pinpoints a definite conjectural equivalence between two deformation functors—one arising from **Riemannian holonomy geometry** and the other from **complex-analytic deformation theory**. The question cuts to the essential interface between **hyperkähler moduli spaces** and their **twistor representations**, asking whether twistor-level and metric-level deformations coincide.  

It is carefully delimited: the projection \( \pi \) and real structure \( \sigma \) are required to be preserved, ensuring that the comparison is between intrinsically linked geometric objects, not arbitrary deformations of the underlying complex manifold. The problem remains open in full generality, despite partial results for K3 surfaces and special higher-dimensional examples, and thus occupies a sharp frontier of current understanding.  

Resolving it would clarify whether the hyperkähler moduli can be described entirely by complex geometry or whether some genuinely differential-geometric information is lost in the twistor translation. Its statement is concise, conceptually elegant, and bridges **Kodaira–Spencer theory**, **Ricci-flat metric moduli**, and **complex geometric rigidity**, epitomizing the sought “narrow entrance, profound depth” ideal of a good research-level problem.

problem:  
Let \((X,d,\mathfrak{m})\) be an \(\mathrm{RCD}(K,N)\) space with \(1<N<\infty\), and let \(V\in W^{1,2}(TX)\) be a measurable vector field whose **regular Lagrangian flow** \((F_t)_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\) exists in the sense of Ambrosio–Gigli–Savaré.  
Assume that \(V\) satisfies the following analytic invariance properties:
\[
\mathrm{div}(V)=0 \quad\text{and}\quad \nabla V = 0 \text{ in } L^2(T\!X\otimes T\!X),
\]
so that \(V\) is an \(L^2\)\,-parallel, divergence‑free vector field in the synthetic sense.

**Question:**  
Does the existence of such a nontrivial \(V\) necessarily imply that \((X,d,\mathfrak{m})\) splits isometrically and measure‑theoretically as a product
\[
(X,d,\mathfrak{m}) \simeq (Y\times\mathbb{R},\, d_Y\oplus |\cdot|,\, \mathfrak{m}_Y\otimes \mathcal{L}^1)
\]
for some \(\mathrm{RCD}(K,N-1)\) space \((Y,d_Y,\mathfrak{m}_Y)\),
with \(V\) corresponding to the unit vector field along the \(\mathbb{R}\)-factor?  
If not, can one construct or even conjecturally describe an \(\mathrm{RCD}(K,N)\) space admitting such a **globally parallel but non‑splitting vector field**?

In other words:  
Is the mere *analytic existence* of a synthetic parallel vector field sufficient to force *geometric splitting*, or can the RCD calculus support “analytic symmetries’’ that are not globally realized by metric lines?

Why is it a "good" problem:  
This version removes the technical ambiguity of tensor pushforwards and invokes only well‑defined notions in the RCD differential calculus—the \(L^2\) covariant derivative and divergence operators.  
The problem is the **synthetic analogue of the classical Cheeger–Gromoll theorem**, but approached from the opposite analytic direction: instead of assuming the existence of a *line* and deducing a parallel field, it assumes a *parallel \(L^2\) vector field* and asks whether the space must split.  

It is a “good” problem because:

1. **Profound Analytic–Geometric Bridge:**  
   It connects first-order calculus (analytic parallelism) with global geometric rigidity (splitting), testing the strength of synthetic differential structures beyond the classical theorem.

2. **Cross‑Field Depth:**  
   A resolution would unify tools from metric measure geometry (regular Lagrangian flow, curvature‑dimension conditions) and analytic geometry (Sobolev modules, divergence, heat flow invariants).  

3. **Unknown Territory:**  
   No known result ensures that \(\nabla V=0\) and \(\mathrm{div}(V)=0\) imply a global line or product beyond the smooth Riemannian case; even the existence of nontrivial such \(V\)'s in non‑smooth RCD spaces is open.

4. **Extreme Simplicity Concealing Depth:**  
   The question is concise—“Does a synthetic parallel vector field imply splitting?”—yet resolving it would expose the core interplay between first‑order analytic symmetries and global geometry in the non‑smooth curvature‑bounded world.

It exemplifies the “narrow entrance, deep interior’’ spirit: an apparently simple analytic condition may or may not enforce one of the most fundamental geometric rigidity phenomena in modern metric geometry.

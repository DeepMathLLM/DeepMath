problem:  
Let \( (M^{m},\omega,g_M,J) \) be a compact Kähler manifold and \( (N,g_N) \) a simply connected Riemannian manifold with sectional curvatures
\[
-b^2 \le K_N \le -a^2 < 0.
\]
Fix a smooth harmonic map \(f_0:(M,g_M)\to(N,g_N)\).  
For a smooth real potential \(\phi\) with zero average, set \(\omega_{\phi}=\omega+i\partial\bar{\partial}\phi\), \(g_{\phi}(\cdot,\cdot)=\omega_{\phi}(\cdot,J\cdot)\), and denote by \( f_{\phi} \) the unique harmonic map close to \(f_0\) taking values in the same homotopy class.  The harmonic energy functional is  
\[
E(\phi) = \tfrac{1}{2}\int_M |df_{\phi}|^2_{g_{\phi},g_N}\,\mathrm{dvol}_{g_{\phi}}, \quad \phi\in C^{\infty}_0(M,\mathbb{R}).
\]

Denote by \(H_{00} = \left.\frac{\delta^2 E}{\delta f^2}\right|_{(f_0,0)}\) the Jacobi operator acting on \(\Gamma(f_0^*TN)\), by \(H_{11} = \left.\frac{\delta^2 E}{\delta \phi^2}\right|_{(f_0,0)}\) the second variation of \(E\) with respect to Kähler potentials (related to the Lichnerowicz operator on functions), and by \(H_{01}=H_{10}^*=\left.\frac{\delta^2 E}{\delta f\,\delta\phi}\right|_{(f_0,0)}\) the mixed second variation capturing how infinitesimal deformations of the Kähler structure change harmonic maps.

Define the block self-adjoint “coupled Hessian”
\[
\mathcal{H}_{f_0} =
\begin{pmatrix}
H_{00} & H_{01}\\
H_{10} & H_{11}
\end{pmatrix}
\]
acting on \( \Gamma(f_0^*TN)\oplus C_0^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})\).

**Problem (Coupled stability gap conjecture):**  
Assume \(K_N\le -a^2<0\).  Does there exist \(\delta=\delta(m,a,b,(M,g_M))>0\) such that for every nonconstant harmonic map \(f_0\),
\[
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{H}_{f_0})\cap(-\delta,\delta)=\{0\},
\]
i.e. the joint Hessian of the harmonic map energy with respect to map and Kähler potential directions has a spectral gap around zero, up to the neutral directions generated by holomorphic isometries of \(M\)?  

Equivalently: can harmonic maps into negatively curved targets ever be *infinitesimally neutrally coupled* to deformations of the domain Kähler metric other than through isometries?

Why is it a "good" problem:  
This revised problem establishes a precise analytic framework by explicitly defining the coupled Hessian operator arising from the mixed second variation of harmonic map energy. It investigates a new notion of *coupled stability*—whether the Bochner–type rigidity enforced by negative curvature persists when the domain’s Kähler structure also varies.  

A positive result would provide the first quantitative measure that negative curvature rigidifies the entire Kähler–harmonic pair, revealing a deep analytic bridge between harmonic map stability, complex deformation theory, and infinite-dimensional Kähler geometry. A counterexample would expose rich “resonances” between the Lichnerowicz spectrum of the domain and the Jacobi spectrum of the map.  

Simple to state yet connecting several sophisticated frameworks—spectral geometry, complex analysis, and nonpositive curvature—this question crystallizes a potentially fundamental interaction law governing stability of geometric energies, thus satisfying the essential criteria of a “good” research problem.

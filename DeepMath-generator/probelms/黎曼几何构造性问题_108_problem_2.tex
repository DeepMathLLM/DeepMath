problem:  
Let \((X,d,\mathfrak{m})\) be an RCD\((K,N)\) metric measure space with \(K\in\mathbb{R}\) and \(1<N<\infty\).  
Fix \(p\in X\) and let \(r_p(x)=d(p,x)\).  
For \(r>0\), define the *average radial mean curvature* by using the coarea formula:
\[
H_p(r):=\frac{d}{dr}\log\mathfrak{m}(B_r(p)),
\]
where \(B_r(p)=\{x\in X:d(p,x)<r\}\) and the derivative is taken in the sense of functions of bounded variation.  
In smooth manifolds with \(\mathrm{Ric}\ge K\) and dimension \(N\), one has
\[
H_p(r)\le (N-1)\frac{\mathrm{sn}'_K(r)}{\mathrm{sn}_K(r)},
\]
and equality characterizes the model spaces of constant curvature \(K\).

**Open problem (quantitative radial Laplacian rigidity):**  
Does there exist a function \(\varepsilon=\varepsilon(K,N)>0\) such that, for any compact RCD\((K,N)\) space,
\[
\Big|H_p(r)-(N-1)\frac{\mathrm{sn}'_K(r)}{\mathrm{sn}_K(r)}\Big|\le \varepsilon(K,N)
\quad\text{for all }p\in X,\ r\in(0,\mathrm{diam}(X)),
\]
implies that \((X,d,\mathfrak{m})\) is Gromov–Hausdorff close to a smooth \(N\)-dimensional Riemannian manifold \((M,g)\) with \(\mathrm{Ric}_g\ge K\)?  
If such an \(\varepsilon\) exists, can it be universal in the sense that it depends only on \(K,N\) and not on \((X,d,\mathfrak{m})\) itself?

Why is it a "good" problem:  
This question reformulates the Laplacian comparison by averaging over metric balls, thereby avoiding the measure-theoretic difficulties of integrating the distributional Laplacian over spheres. The function \(H_p(r)=\frac{d}{dr}\log\mathfrak{m}(B_r(p))\) is well defined in RCD spaces via coarea disintegration and exactly captures the *average radial divergence* of distance functions.  

The problem seeks a quantitative stability principle linking this one-dimensional averaged curvature quantity to global geometric regularity — a metric analogue of Cheeger–Colding almost rigidity, but built on Laplacian comparison instead of volume ratios. It questions whether near-equality in the analytic form of Ricci lower bounds forces the space to be metrically close to a smooth manifold, integrating analytic curvature comparison, measure concentration, and synthetic geometry in one framework.  

Resolving it would extend the rigidity and stability program in RCD theory, clarifying how local analytic curvature control encoded by \(H_p(r)\) determines large-scale geometry, and forging a new quantitative bridge between PDE-inspired curvature bounds and global metric structure.

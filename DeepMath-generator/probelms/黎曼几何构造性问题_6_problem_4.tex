problem:  
Let \( (S^{n+k}, g_{S^{n+k}}) \) be the unit round sphere and  
\(\{M_i^n\subset S^{n+k}\}\) be a sequence of smooth, compact, embedded **minimal submanifolds** satisfying the critical curvature and volume bounds  
\[
\int_{M_i} |A_{M_i}|^n \le C, \qquad \mathrm{Vol}(M_i)\le V_0.
\]  
Assume (after subsequence) that \(M_i\rightharpoonup M_\infty\) as varifolds, with smooth convergence away from singular points \(\Sigma=\{p_1,\dots,p_\ell\}\).  
Blow-up sequences around each singular point \(p_j\) yield complete embedded minimal submanifolds  
\(\Sigma_j^\ast\subset \mathbb{R}^{n+k+1}\) with finite total curvature  
\[
E(\Sigma_j^\ast):=\int_{\Sigma_j^\ast} |A_{\Sigma_j^\ast}|^n\, d\mu_{\Sigma_j^\ast} <\infty.
\]

**Question (Discreteness of bubbling energies at the critical curvature scale):**  
Is the set  
\[
\mathcal{E}_{n,k}:=\Big\{\int_{\Sigma^\ast} |A_{\Sigma^\ast}|^n : \Sigma^\ast \text{ complete, embedded, minimal, finite-total-curvature in }\mathbb{R}^{n+k+1}\Big\}
\]  
a discrete subset of \( \mathbb{R} \)?  

Equivalently, in any bubble-tree convergence of minimal submanifolds in the sphere with bounded \(L^n\)-curvature, do the blow-up energies satisfy a **quantization law**
\[
\int_{M_i}|A_{M_i}|^n \to \int_{M_\infty}|A_{M_\infty}|^n + \sum_{j=1}^\ell \Xi_j,
\]
where each \(\Xi_j := E(\Sigma_j^\ast)\in \mathcal{E}_{n,k}\) comes from this discrete set of “elementary curvature quanta”?  

If not discrete, can one construct families of complete minimal blow-ups with continuous variation of total curvature energy, implying that curvature losses in the critical regime can vary continuously rather than quantized?

Why is it a "good" problem:  
This question isolates a sharp unknown mechanism in **critical geometry**: what structure governs energy concentration when curvature is exactly scale-invariant?  While the general bubble-tree decomposition is known, the nature of the **energy spectrum of bubbles**—discrete versus continuous—remains conceptually open.  
A positive answer would uncover a universality principle: all curvature loss at the analytic threshold occurs in finite, quantized packets reflecting an intrinsic “atomic” structure of minimal curvature energy—analogous to quantization phenomena in quantum field–type PDEs.  
A negative answer would reveal novel continuous moduli of minimal bubbles, exposing deep analytical flexibility in critical geometric systems.  
The question connects geometric measure theory, elliptic PDE analysis, and the global classification of complete minimal submanifolds. It is simple to state, rests on well-defined geometric invariants, but reaches one of the most delicate frontiers linking regularity, blow-up analysis, and geometric quantization—thus fitting the profile of a genuinely "good" research-level problem.

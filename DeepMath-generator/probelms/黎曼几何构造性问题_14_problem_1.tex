problem:  
Let \( (N^{2n}, g, J) \) denote the complex projective space \( \mathbb{C}P^n \) equipped with the Fubini–Study metric of constant holomorphic sectional curvature \( c > 0 \).  
Let \( \mathbb{R}P^n \subset \mathbb{C}P^n \) be the standard totally geodesic real form, which is a minimal Lagrangian submanifold satisfying \( \lambda_1(\mathbb{R}P^n) = n c \), where \( \lambda_1(L) \) denotes the first nonzero eigenvalue of the Laplace–Beltrami operator on a compact Riemannian manifold \(L\).

**Conjectural Rigidity Problem:**  
Fix \( \varepsilon > 0 \). Determine whether there exists \( \varepsilon_0 > 0 \) depending only on \(n\) such that the following holds:  

> If \( L^n \subset \mathbb{C}P^n \) is a compact, embedded, minimal Lagrangian submanifold satisfying  
> (1) \( \big| \lambda_1(L) - n c \big| < \varepsilon_0 \), and  
> (2) \( \mathrm{Vol}(L) \le (1 + \varepsilon_0)\, \mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb{R}P^n) \),  
>  
> then \(L\) must be congruent to \(\mathbb{R}P^n\) under the action of the holomorphic isometry group \(\mathrm{PU}(n+1)\).  

Equivalently, does a simultaneous *spectral and volumetric pinching* toward the standard real projective subspace force geometric rigidity among compact minimal Lagrangian submanifolds of \(\mathbb{C}P^n\)?

Why is it a "good" problem:  
This problem is a **quantitative rigidity conjecture** linking eigenvalue spectrum, volume, and minimal Lagrangian geometry in a symmetric Kähler manifold.  
Its strength lies in bridging three deep areas:  

1. **Spectral Geometry:** The eigenvalue pinching condition probes whether analytic proximity in the Laplacian spectrum can determine global geometry, mirroring Lichnerowicz–Obata type rigidity but within the Lagrangian setting.  

2. **Calibrated and Symplectic Geometry:** Minimal Lagrangian submanifolds are calibrated objects—critical points of the volume functional constrained by the symplectic structure—and their stability relates to Hamiltonian variations. The problem thus asks how spectral invariants control Hamiltonian stability.  

3. **Geometric Rigidity and Symmetric Spaces:** The conjecture extends classical sphere theorems and volume pinching results to complex space forms, testing whether the real form \(\mathbb{R}P^n\) is spectrally and volumetrically isolated.  

The statement is simple, quantitative, and geometrically natural, yet deeply unknown: even determining the appropriate small constant \(\varepsilon_0\) would require novel synthesis of spectral comparison, second variation analysis, and calibrated rigidity techniques. It thus exemplifies the “narrow entrance, profound depth’’ principle of a good mathematical problem.

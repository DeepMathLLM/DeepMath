problem:  
Let \( X \) be a compact Calabi–Yau manifold with complex structure \( J \), holomorphic volume form \( \Omega \), and a Ricci-flat Kähler form \( \omega_0 \).  
Let \( L_0 \subset X \) be a compact special Lagrangian submanifold with respect to \( (\omega_0, \Omega) \).  
By McLean’s theorem, the local deformation space of special Lagrangians near \( L_0 \) inside \( (X,\omega_0,\Omega) \) is a smooth manifold \( \mathcal{M}_{SLag}(L_0) \) of dimension \( b^1(L_0) \).  

Let \( \mathcal{K} \) denote the Kähler cone of \( X \). For each nearby Kähler class \( [\omega] \in \mathcal{K} \), let \( g_\omega \) denote the unique Ricci-flat metric in that class, as guaranteed by Yau’s theorem.

**Problem:**  
Is there a local smooth correspondence between the Kähler moduli near \([\omega_0]\) and the McLean moduli of \(L_0\) — that is, does there exist a smooth map  
\[
\Phi : (\mathcal{K}, [\omega_0]) \longrightarrow \mathcal{M}_{SLag}(L_0)
\]
defined near \([\omega_0]\), such that for each nearby \([\omega]\), the image \(L_{[\omega]} = \Phi([\omega])\) is a special Lagrangian submanifold of \((X, \omega, \Omega)\) representing the same homology class as \(L_0\)?  

Equivalently, at the infinitesimal level, does there exist a nontrivial linear coupling between the tangent space \(T_{[\omega_0]} \mathcal{K}\) (the space of harmonic \((1,1)\)-forms representing infinitesimal Kähler deformations) and the tangent space \(T_{L_0}\mathcal{M}_{SLag}(L_0)\) (space of harmonic 1-forms on \(L_0\)) that preserves the special Lagrangian calibration condition to first order?

Why is it a "good" problem:  
This problem probes the unknown analytic interface between two of the most fundamental deformation theories in Calabi–Yau geometry: the deformation of the ambient Ricci-flat Kähler metric (Yau theory) and the deformation of special Lagrangian submanifolds (McLean theory). Each is well understood in isolation, yet their mutual interaction—how a small change in the ambient Kähler class might necessitate or induce a corresponding deformation of special Lagrangian representatives—remains largely uncharted.

Establishing even a local or infinitesimal coupling \(\Phi\) would represent a major advance in our understanding of special Lagrangian fibrations and the geometric mechanisms underlying the Strominger–Yau–Zaslow (SYZ) conjecture. It would reveal how the calibrated geometry of the real side of mirror symmetry is encoded in the complex–Kähler moduli interplay, thereby bridging complex geometry, symplectic geometry, and geometric analysis.  

The formulation is simple—a small neighborhood problem linking two finite-dimensional moduli—but its resolution demands deep insights from nonlinear PDEs, Hodge theory, and calibrated geometry. The simplicity of the statement, the conceptual power of its implications, and its potential to synthesize distinct geometric deformation theories make it a “good” and profound research problem.

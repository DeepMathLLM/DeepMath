problem:  
Let \((M,\Delta,g)\) be a **compact equiregular sub-Riemannian manifold** with smooth bracket-generating distribution \(\Delta \subset TM\) and sub-Riemannian metric \(g\).  
Denote by \(\mathcal{A}\) the set of all **abnormal covectors** in \(T^*M\), i.e.
\[
\mathcal{A} := \{\lambda(0) \in T^*M\setminus\{0\} \mid \exists\, \lambda(\cdot) \text{ satisfying Pontryagin’s equations with } \langle \lambda(t), X_i(\pi(\lambda(t)))\rangle \equiv 0 \text{ for all } i\}.
\]
Assume that the **regular stratum** \(\Sigma_{\mathrm{reg}}\subset \mathcal{A}\)—the subset where \(\mathcal{A}\) is a smooth conic submanifold of locally constant codimension—is nonempty.  

Let \(\theta\) be the canonical Liouville 1-form on \(T^*M\), with symplectic form \(\omega=d\theta\).  
Define, for \(\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathrm{reg}}\), the **characteristic distribution**
\[
\mathcal{C}_\lambda := \{v\in T_\lambda\Sigma_{\mathrm{reg}} : \omega(v,w)=0 \text{ for all } w\in T_\lambda\Sigma_{\mathrm{reg}}\}.
\]
This is the degeneracy distribution of the restricted symplectic form \(\omega|_{\Sigma_{\mathrm{reg}}}\).

**Question:**  
In the space of \(C^\infty\) sub-Riemannian metrics \(g\) on the fixed distribution \(\Delta\), endowed with the Whitney topology, is it generic that:

1. the regular abnormal covector set \(\Sigma_{\mathrm{reg}}\) is a **coisotropic submanifold** of \((T^*M,\omega)\), and the integral curves of \(\mathcal{C}\) project under \(\pi:T^*M\to M\) to the abnormal extremals of \((\Delta,g)\);  
or  
2. \(\Sigma_{\mathrm{reg}}\) is **nowhere coisotropic**, meaning \(\omega|_{\Sigma_{\mathrm{reg}}}\) has maximal rank and admits no nontrivial characteristic directions?  

Less restrictively, does there exist an open and dense subset of metrics for which the set of coisotropic points of \(\Sigma_{\mathrm{reg}}\) is either empty or forms an open smooth stratum corresponding to a canonical foliation whose projected leaves are abnormal geodesics?

Why is it a "good" problem:  
This formulation achieves rigorous clarity while preserving the conceptual depth:  

1. **Profound insight:**  
   It isolates a fundamental symplectic-geometric property of abnormal sets—coisotropy—as the governing condition that might distinguish geometrically integrable from “chaotic” abnormal dynamics. Resolving this would clarify whether there exists an intrinsic symplectic–geometric regularity principle underlying abnormal extremals.  

2. **Bridge between fields:**  
   The problem unites *sub-Riemannian control geometry* with *symplectic and contact geometry*. Determining whether \(\Sigma_{\mathrm{reg}}\) generically becomes coisotropic invites techniques from Poisson geometry, transversality, and symplectic singularity theory to interface directly with control theory.  

3. **Extreme simplicity:**  
   The statement is minimalistic—“is the regular abnormal covector set generically coisotropic, or nowhere so?”—yet its resolution requires deep tools and may reveal new universal geometric behaviors of control singularities.  

Thus, the problem fulfills all criteria of a *good research-level question*: it is elegantly posed but leads into intricate structures at the intersection of symplectic geometry, control theory, and singular analysis, pointing toward genuinely unexplored frontiers.

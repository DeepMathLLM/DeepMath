problem:  
Let \(M^n\) be a complete, noncompact Riemannian manifold with nonnegative sectional curvature, and let \(S \subset M\) be its soul (by the Cheeger–Gromoll Soul Theorem).  
For \(r > 0\), denote by  
\[
\Sigma_r = \{ x \in M \mid \operatorname{dist}(S,x) = r \}
\]
the distance hypersurface from \(S\).  

Assume that for all sufficiently large \(r\):  
1. \(\Sigma_r\) is smooth and **strictly convex** with respect to the outward normal (all principal curvatures positive);  
2. There exist scaling factors \(\lambda_r = r^{-1}\) such that the rescaled hypersurfaces \((\Sigma_r, \lambda_r^2 g|_{\Sigma_r})\) subconverge smoothly (in the \(C^\infty\) Cheeger–Gromov sense) to a compact Riemannian manifold \((\Sigma_\infty, g_\infty)\).  

**Question:**  
Does the limiting manifold \((\Sigma_\infty, g_\infty)\) necessarily have nonnegative sectional curvature and carry the structure of a smooth **Riemannian sphere bundle** over \(S\)?  
Equivalently, does strict convexity of large distance spheres force the Sharafutdinov retraction \(\pi : M \to S\) to be **asymptotically a Riemannian submersion** with round fibers at infinity?  
If not, can one construct a nonnegatively curved counterexample?  

Why is it a "good" problem:  
This problem asks whether *convex geometric control at infinity* determines the **asymptotic fibered structure** of noncompact manifolds with \(\sec \ge 0\). It lies at the intersection of **comparison geometry**, **asymptotic analysis**, and **bundle topology**.  

It is well founded mathematically: the assumption of smooth convergence avoids regularity issues of Gromov–Hausdorff limits, so notions such as sectional curvature and bundle structure become meaningful. The question now precisely tests whether the Riccati evolution for the shape operator, combined with global nonnegative curvature, forces a form of **asymptotic rigidity**—namely, whether Euclidean normal expansion induces a round fiber geometry in the limit.  

The problem is “good” in the sense of being both conceptually simple and profoundly deep:
- The setup invokes only the distance function from the soul and convexity of level sets, which are geometrically elementary notions.
- Yet answering it would require new insights into the interaction between **extrinsic convexity**, **curvature decay**, and **metric submersion rigidity**—topics not fully understood even in classical comparison geometry.
  
A resolution would illuminate how the intrinsic and extrinsic geometries of the soul determine the global asymptotic shape of \(M\), potentially leading to new rigidity or stability results for nonnegatively curved manifolds and providing new bridges between **Riccati analysis**, **Sharafutdinov retraction theory**, and **asymptotic geometry**.

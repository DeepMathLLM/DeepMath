problem:  
Let \((M, \Delta, g)\) be a smooth, equiregular sub-Riemannian manifold with Hausdorff measure \(\mu\).  
Suppose that the metric tangent cone \(\operatorname{Tan}(M, d, \mu, x)\) at every point \(x \in M\) is isomorphic, as a metric measure space, to a fixed Carnot group \((G, d_G, \mu_G)\).  
Assume further that \((M, d, \mu)\) satisfies a uniform measure-contraction-type condition \(MCP(K,N)\) compatible with the sub-Riemannian structure (e.g., that of Juillet, Rizzi, and Agrachev).  

**Open problem:**  
Does there exist a nontrivial (non-Riemannian) equiregular sub-Riemannian manifold satisfying these hypotheses for which \((M, d, \mu)\) is *not* locally isomorphic to the Carnot group \((G, d_G, \mu_G)\)?  
Equivalently, do *constant sub-Riemannian tangent geometry* and a *synthetic measure-contraction bound* together force local homogeneity, or can one build globally curved examples whose infinitesimal models are the same homogeneous Carnot group everywhere?

Why is it a "good" problem:  
This revised problem avoids the rigidity already known for \(CD(K,N)\) spaces, replacing it with the sub-Riemannian-compatible condition \(MCP(K,N)\), under which nontrivial examples (such as the Heisenberg group) naturally live. It probes the boundary between **local infinitesimal homogeneity** (constant Carnot tangents) and **global analytic curvature phenomena** (those captured by the measure-contraction property). Known examples—contact manifolds with Heisenberg tangents—show that constant nilpotent approximation alone does not enforce homogeneity, but the interaction with synthetic curvature bounds remains elusive.  

Resolving this question would illuminate whether curvature-type conditions in metric measure geometry constrain the global topology or geometry of sub-Riemannian spaces, unifying aspects of geometric measure theory, sub-Riemannian differential geometry, and analysis on metric spaces. The problem is elegantly simple in statement yet conceptually deep, aiming to reveal whether infinitesimal geometric rigidity propagates globally under analytic curvature constraints.

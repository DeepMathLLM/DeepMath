problem:  
Let \( \{(M_i^n,g_i)\} \) be a noncollapsing sequence of compact, simply connected Riemannian manifolds with nonnegative sectional curvature converging in the Gromov–Hausdorff sense to a compact Alexandrov space \( X \) of curvature \( \ge 0 \).  
Assume that every space of directions \( \Sigma_x \) in \( X \) is homeomorphic to a smooth, simply connected closed manifold and that this homeomorphism type is independent of \( x \).  

Suppose furthermore that each \( \Sigma_x \) is **rationally elliptic**, i.e. it has finite-dimensional rational cohomology and finite-dimensional rational homotopy groups:
\[
\dim_\mathbb Q H^*(\Sigma_x;\mathbb Q) <\infty, \quad
\dim_\mathbb Q \pi_*(\Sigma_x)\otimes \mathbb Q < \infty.
\]
  
**Question:**  
Does it follow that, for all sufficiently large \( i \), the smooth approximating manifolds \( M_i \) are themselves rationally elliptic?  
Equivalently, can the pointwise rational ellipticity of tangent directions in a nonnegatively curved Alexandrov limit enforce global rational ellipticity on all nearby smooth manifolds?  
  
If this is not always true, under what additional geometric or topological hypotheses—such as regularity of the limit stratification, uniform metric pinching, or controlled holonomy variation—does this passage from local ellipticity to global ellipticity hold?  

Why is it a "good" problem:  
This formulation preserves mathematical precision while probing a deep, unexplored terrain linking rational homotopy finiteness with curvature-limit geometry. It articulates a concrete local-to-global mechanism for Bott’s conjecture in the Alexandrov–limit setting: if every infinitesimal model (space of directions) is already rationally elliptic, must nearby nonnegatively curved manifolds share that homotopical rigidity? The problem stands at the interface of Riemannian convergence theory, Alexandrov geometry, and rational homotopy theory, drawing techniques from each. A positive result would suggest that rational ellipticity is stable under geometrically controlled degeneration in nonnegative curvature, offering a new path toward Bott’s vision; a counterexample would challenge our understanding of local–global interactions in the topology of curved spaces. Its simplicity of statement belies profound depth and cross-disciplinary potential, making it an exemplary “good” research problem.

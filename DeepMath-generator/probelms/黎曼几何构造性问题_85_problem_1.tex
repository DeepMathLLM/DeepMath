problem:  
Let \((M^n, g(t))_{t\in(-\infty,0]}\) be a complete ancient solution to the Ricci flow with bounded curvature and nonnegative curvature operator. Assume further that for every sequence \(t_k \to -\infty\), the rescaled flows \((M, |t_k|^{-1} g(|t_k|t))\) subconverge in the Cheeger–Gromov sense to a gradient shrinking soliton \((N^n, h(t))\).  

**Open problem:**  
Must \((M, g(t))\) itself evolve self-similarly, i.e. be a gradient shrinking Ricci soliton up to scaling and diffeomorphism?  
Equivalently, does asymptotic self-similarity of blow-down limits at \(t\to -\infty\) force *global* self-similarity of the ancient flow, without assuming κ-noncollapsing or dimension restrictions?  

Why is it a "good" problem:  
This problem refines the rigidity expectations governing ancient Ricci flows and avoids asserting an overly strong structural classification (such as prescribing the product \(S^k\times\mathbb{R}^{n-k}\)) that may not hold in higher dimensions. The question asks whether the existence of asymptotically solitonic blow-down limits guarantees that the entire flow is itself a soliton—testing the rigidity of the Ricci flow evolution under geometric convergence hypotheses.  

This formulation lies between known theorems and genuinely open questions. In dimensions two and three, Perelman’s and Hamilton’s work, combined with Brendle’s rigidity results for κ-solutions, confirm self-similarity in specific cases. However, it remains completely open whether such rigidity extends to higher dimensions or without κ-noncollapsing assumptions.  

The depth of the problem arises from its intersection of analytic and geometric mechanisms: the monotonicity of Perelman’s \(\mathcal{W}\)-entropy and reduced volume, geometric limits of backward parabolic rescalings, and the structural stability of gradient solitons. A resolution would reveal whether the ultimate asymptotic geometry of an ancient flow entirely governs its global evolution—bridging the gap between asymptotic analysis, Ricci soliton theory, and potential canonical form classifications under Ricci flow.  

Its statement is conceptually elementary and directly related to the geometric intuition behind singularity models (“ancient = eternally self-similar?”), yet its resolution would require deep new understanding of analytic rigidity and geometric limit flows. This balance of simplicity, depth, and cross-field connection renders it a good and profound research problem.

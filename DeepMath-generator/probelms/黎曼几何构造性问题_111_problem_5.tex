problem:  
Let \((M,g,V)\) be a complete smooth weighted Riemannian manifold with \(\mu=e^{-V}\,d\mathrm{vol}_g\) and Bakry–Émery Ricci tensor \(\mathrm{Ric}_V=\mathrm{Ric}_g+\nabla^2V\).  
Let \((X_t)_{t\ge0}\) denote the diffusion generated by the weighted Laplacian \(\Delta_V=\Delta-\nabla V\cdot\nabla\), and \(U_t:T_xM\to T_{X_t}M\) its stochastic parallel transport.  

Fix a finite time scale \(t_0>0\).  
Define the **finite-time averaged Bakry–Émery curvature functional**  
\[
\mathbf{R}_{t_0}(x,v)
:= \frac{1}{t_0}\,\mathbb{E}_x\!\left[\int_0^{t_0} \mathrm{Ric}_V(X_s)(U_s v,U_s v)\, ds \right],
\quad v\in T_xM,\ |v|=1.
\]
Suppose that for some constant \(K>0\) one has  
\[
\mathbf{R}_{t_0}(x,v)\ge K
\qquad\forall\,x\in M,\ |v|=1.
\]
(The inequality is imposed only on this **integrated stochastic average**, not at each instant \(s<t_0\).)

Let \((P_t)_{t>0}\) denote the diffusion semigroup, and for smooth positive \(f\) define the entropy functional  
\(\mathrm{Ent}_\mu(f)=\int f\log f\,d\mu\)  
and the entropy dissipation rate  
\[
\mathcal{E}_t(f)=-\frac{d}{dt}\big[\mathrm{Ent}_\mu(P_t f)\big]
   = \int |\nabla \log P_t f|^2\,P_t f\,d\mu.
\]

**Question (research-level open problem):**  
Assuming the finite-time average bound above, does there exist a universal function \(\eta(n,t_0)\to0\) as \(t_0\to0\) such that for all smooth positive \(f\),
\[
\mathcal{E}_t(f)
   \le e^{-2(K-\eta(n,t_0))t}\,\mathcal{E}_0(f)
   \qquad \text{for all }t\ge t_0?
\]
Equivalently, are log-Sobolev or exponential entropy-contraction inequalities *stable under curvature lower bounds that hold only after curvature is averaged* along diffusion trajectories over a fixed time window?  
If such stability fails in general, can one characterize geometric or analytic conditions under which it holds, and identify the minimal temporal scale \(t_0>0\) ensuring effective exponential decay?

---

Why is it a "good" problem:  
This problem articulates a precise and mathematically sound weakening of the classical Bakry–Émery lower curvature bound, replacing pointwise infinitesimal control with **finite-time stochastic averaging** along diffusion paths.  
It poses a fundamental and open question: does the deep link between curvature and the dissipative behavior of the heat flow survive when curvature is known only in this averaged, probabilistic sense?  

Conceptually, it defines a new curvature notion at finite temporal resolution and asks whether analytic curvature–dimension consequences (entropy decay, log-Sobolev inequalities) extend robustly to this “coarse stochastic” context.  
Such a result would bridge geometric analysis, stochastic calculus, and information geometry, potentially leading to a *multi-scale curvature–dimension theory*.  

The problem is easy to state yet profound in scope, demanding new quantitative links between stochastic transport, Bochner identities at finite scale, and functional inequalities.  
It perfectly embodies the “narrow entrance, profound depth” criterion of a good research problem: a simple formulation that could open a new chapter in the understanding of curvature and diffusion.

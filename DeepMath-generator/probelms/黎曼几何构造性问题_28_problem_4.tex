problem:  
Let \((M^{2n}, g, J)\) be a compact Kähler manifold with **positive scalar curvature**, and \((N,h)\) a compact Riemannian manifold with **nonpositive sectional curvature**.  It is known through Eells–Sampson that if \(\mathrm{Ric}_M>0\) then every harmonic map \(u:M\to N\) is constant.  However, the transition from pointwise Ricci positivity to **weaker, integrated, or almost-Einstein conditions** is not understood.  

Define the **minimal negative Ricci ratio**
\[
\Lambda(g) := \frac{\int_M (\max\{0,-\lambda_{\min}(\mathrm{Ric}_M)\})^2\, dV_g}{\int_M \mathrm{Scal}_M^2\, dV_g},
\]
where \(\lambda_{\min}(\mathrm{Ric}_M)\) is the smallest eigenvalue of the Ricci tensor at each point.  Thus \(\Lambda(g)\) measures how much of the Ricci tensor’s negativity remains relative to its scalar positivity.

**Question:**  
Does there exist a universal constant \(C_n>0\) (depending only on complex dimension \(n\)) such that if a compact Kähler \((M^{2n},g,J)\) satisfies \(\mathrm{Scal}_M>0\) and \(\Lambda(g)<C_n\), then every harmonic map \(u:(M,g)\to(N,h)\) into any nonpositively curved \(N\) must be constant?  
Equivalently, does *quantitatively small total negativity* of the Ricci spectrum, measured against scalar curvature positivity, still enforce harmonic rigidity?

Why is it a "good" problem:  
This question is both **well-posed** and **deeply open**: it asks whether *almost* Ricci-positivity in an integrated spectral sense suffices for harmonic map rigidity, a fundamentally analytic–geometric link not captured by current Bochner arguments.  It is not a repetition of known theorems—Eells–Sampson rigidity requires pointwise Ricci positivity, while energy identities afford no control when only the scalar curvature is positive.  A positive resolution would establish a new quantitative bridge between weak curvature dominance (captured by \(L^2\)-type Ricci bounds) and global analytic rigidity.  Conversely, counterexamples would delineate the sharp boundary of the Bochner method’s reach for Kähler domains.  The problem thus directly targets a subtle, unexplored threshold between local curvature positivity and global analytic behavior—simple to state yet profound in geometric analysis, embodying the qualities of a “good” research-level problem.
